visuals:
Y2K / early 2000s graphic design / Liquid Digital

Translucent layering with depth — images bleed through each other at low opacity. Water is the core texture: droplets on glass, ocean surfaces, rain, caustics. Everything feels slightly submerged.

Color palette sits in a narrow teal-cyan-blue-green range with desaturated earth tones (mossy greens, muted browns) as secondary. Blacks are deep. Whites are blown out and hazy, never crisp.

Light behaves like it's refracting — soft radial flares, glowing edges, diffused lens effects. Nothing is evenly lit. Illumination comes from within or behind elements.

Organic forms dominate geometry: spheres, bubbles, curved waveforms, botanical shapes (ferns, petals, leaves). When grids or lines appear they're degraded — scratched, partially dissolved, interrupted by organic texture.

Typography is lowercase, thin, clean sans-serif. Sits quietly against busy backgrounds. Sometimes slightly transparent itself. Never shouts — it floats.

Textures are layered: macro nature photography + digital noise + scratched metal + water + light. The result feels simultaneously natural and synthetic — like looking at a forest through a wet screen.

The overall mood is nocturnal, subaquatic, contemplative. Early-2000s electronic music culture filtered through Photoshop compositing. Cool but not cold. Digital but not sterile.
